Research

My broad research interests currently lie in multi-agent decision-making and control in constrained and uncertain environments, spanning cooperative, competitive, adversarial, and game-theoretic interactions. Drawing on tools from geometric and Lyapunov-based analysis, model predictive control, safety-critical control, and game-theoretic control, my doctoral research has addressed multi-agent pursuit–evasion problems, with a focus on designing strategies that guarantee encirclement and finite-time capture in constrained environments.

I am working on (or have worked on) the following interesting problems:

Simulation / video coming soon

Encirclement guaranteed finite-time capture against unknown evader strategies

A team of pursuers must keep an evader inside their convex hull and capture it in finite time without knowing the evader’s strategy, for example surveillance robots surrounding an intruder in a monitored area. We design geometric pursuit strategies that maintain encirclement throughout the game and prove finite-time capture using a Lyapunov-based analysis, with an explicit bound on the capture time.

[J1] · arXiv

Simulation / video coming soon

3D cooperative pursuit with guaranteed enclosure via robust model predictive control

In three dimensions the evader has many more ways to escape. We partition the evader-centred space so that enclosure is guaranteed whenever each pursuer stays in its assigned region, and each pursuer solves a decentralized robust MPC problem that treats the evader’s motion as uncertainty.

[C1]

Simulation / video coming soon

Finite-time capture by a Dubins pursuer

A Dubins pursuer moves at constant speed with a bounded turn rate, while a slower evader can change its heading instantly. We propose a turn-rate feedback law with a single tunable gain that guarantees finite-time capture against any admissible evader strategy without violating the turn-rate limit, together with an explicit capture-time bound.

[S1] · arXiv

Simulation / video coming soon

Model predictive control based target defense

A defender must protect a target from an attacker without knowing the target’s location or the attacker’s strategy. The defender predicts the attacker’s future trajectory online from its recent motion and uses this prediction within an MPC formulation, including in the presence of obstacles.

[C2]

Simulation / video coming soon

Robust receding-horizon games

Multiple agents with coupled constraints each solve their own predictive control problem under bounded disturbances. Using tube-based constraint tightening, we guarantee recursive feasibility and show convergence to a neighbourhood of a generalized Nash equilibrium.

[W1] · arXiv
